Date: 2026-10-01. Branch `m6` @ `d75bebd`.

- Full corpus (`--ext dds,nif`, 6.49 GB, 100,754 blocks of 64 KiB).

## What each task contributed

Gains are interleaved A/B ratios against the previous task's code, from the task reports.

| Task | Change | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Register diet (about 87 → 70 registers per thread), u16 price tables, workgroup arrays trimmed per pass | 4080 blocks fit one wave. K3 −3.5 % at b2900 and −24 % at max (+24 % MB/s at max) |
| A2 | Rep-length memo (a match at offset o, length L also gives L−d at p+d), flat sequence emit | K3 −5 % |
| A3 | Dead-position skip: 61 % of positions share no 3-byte prefix with any earlier position. K2opt marks them and K3 jumps over them | K3 −17…−21 %, +14…+19 % MB/s |
| A4 | Persistent K3: workgroups loop over blocks taken heaviest-first from a GPU-sorted order | +2…+5 % on the 5090; **K3 −36 % on a 4060 proxy** (3 blocks/SM) |

Rejected along the way, with measurements in the task reports:
- lockstep rep extension (spills registers);
- grid relaxation in the final pass (+4 %);
- per-tile dead runs (+12 % K2);
- `wgpu_int16` (neutral).

## Why A4 matters off the 5090

On the 5090, a batch nearly fits one wave, so heavy-first ordering has little to fix. On 8 GB cards every batch is many waves, and NVIDIA's Vulkan driver starts each wave only after the previous one's slowest block finishes. The persistent kernel removes that wait. The 4060 proxy pads workgroup memory so only 3 blocks fit per SM, and under it K3 went from 127 to 81 µs/block. That is a projection for real 8 GB cards, not a measurement on one.
