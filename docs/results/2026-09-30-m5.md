# M5 results: libzstd L14 / L16 ratio on the GPU (`opt14`, `opt16`)

Date: 2026-09-30 · Branch `m5` (T5 on top of `31d5d72`) · **Block 64 KiB** (also validated at 32 and 16 KiB) ·
Budget 6144 MiB · RTX 5090, full corpus (`--ext dds,nif`, 6.49 GB, 100754 blocks at 64 KiB), `--inflight 3`,
medians of 3.

The GPU was idle apart from a remote-desktop client (`parsecd`, 827 MiB, idle). `nvidia-smi` was sampled every
0.5 s during each timed run and saw no other compute process. The host was not idle: two QEMU VMs and a
`haskill` process used 1–1.6 cores (load average 5–30). The GPU runs are kernel-bound (the kernel sum equals the
wall time to within 1 %), so the host load barely shows in them. The libzstd numbers share the machine with it.

- Every GPU frame is byte-identical to the CPU oracle.
  - `gpu --verify` (libzstd decodes every frame) passed on the full corpus at 64, 32 and 16 KiB.
  - At each block size, `gpu`'s compressed bytes equal `ref`'s exactly. At 64 KiB: opt14 4737822257, opt16 4735028925.
  - The per-frame differential tests pass too (see Correctness).
- `opt14` delivers libzstd L14's ratio at 3.75× libzstd's 32-thread speed. `opt16` delivers L16's at 2.87×.
  `opt14` is 0.027 % short of L16.
- Both are quality presets: 4.8× (`opt14`) and 7.3× (`opt16`) slower than `lvl9s12seg`, for +2.3 % / +2.4 % ratio.
- **`--batch max` crosses the one-wave boundary.** With the direct upload the 6 GiB budget allows 3586 blocks
  (3458 with the copy upload), but K3opt fits about 3400 blocks per wave on the 5090 (T3b). The second, partial
  wave costs 20 % (opt14) and 24 % (opt16).
  - `--batch max` keeps its meaning (the largest batch that fits the VRAM budget), so both figures are above.
  - Single runs near the boundary: b3200 gave 2237 / 1475 MB/s and b3400 gave 2248 / 1487 MB/s (opt14 / opt16).

- K3 splits by pass as follows (the `k3opt_passes_timing` harness, 2900 corpus blocks, same session):
  - `opt14`: 9.09 (Prior-seeded cheap pass), 9.28 (final) and 0.27 (fix-up) µs/block;
  - `opt16`: 8.23, 8.17, 8.24 (three cheap passes), 9.20 (final) and 0.26 µs/block.
- K3opt is 61 % (`opt14`) and 75 % (`opt16`) of the GPU time.
- K1 is the next largest, at 23 % of `opt14`. Two chains cost as much as `lvl3`'s two chains (T2 log).
- Against the design's projection (`m5-opt-design.md` §3.3):
  - `opt14`: 29.3 µs/block against 16–18, so 2.17 GB/s against 3.6–4.0;
  - `opt16`: 44.7 µs/block against 21–25, so 1.43 GB/s against 2.7–3.1.
  - The gap is K3opt: 9 µs per pass instead of the projected 3–4 (T3/T3b logs). K1 is also 6.9 µs instead of 5.4.

## Correctness

- **Pipeline differential tests** (`crates/gzc-gpu/tests/opt_pipeline.rs`, and `pipeline.rs` unit tests):
  - frames and parses byte-identical to the oracle;
  - covered: `compress_batch` (parse path), `compress_frames`, and the streaming `Pipeline` in all four modes
    (copy / direct upload × main-queue / transfer-queue readback), with partial batches and reuse;
  - the zero-copy API with partial submissions over stale slot bytes;
  - `allocated_bytes == vram_bytes` in every mode.
- **4000 uniform-stride corpus blocks** (`opt_corpus_matches_oracle`, ignored), for opt14 and opt16:
  - every frame equal in all four modes, at 64 KiB and at 16 KiB;
  - run both with subgroups and with `GZC_NO_SUBGROUPS=1`.
- **Full suites:**
  - `gzc-gpu` at 64 KiB, at 64 KiB with `GZC_NO_SUBGROUPS=1`, and at 16 KiB;
  - `gzc-core`;
  - `gzc-bench`.
  - Existing presets stay byte-identical: the lvl3 anchor and all differential tests pass unchanged.
- **3-byte nbSeq form:** unreachable for the optimal parse at the block sizes it runs at.
  - `MAX_SEQS_OPT` = 21846 < 0x7F00 = 32512 at 64 KiB, so a 64 KiB block never has enough sequences. A const
    assert in `compressor.rs` pins this.
  - The form itself stays tested at the CPU level (`gzc_core::seqenc` tests, `0x7F00` and `0x7F00 + 0x123`) and
    on the GPU at 128 KiB (`differential.rs`).

## What T5 changed (integration)

- **`gpu_supports`** accepts `opt` params at blocks of at most 64 KiB.
  - `Kernels` builds K2opt as its K2 and `OptPasses` (wg16, the workgroup ring) as its K3, on the shared
    `BatchBuffers`: `best` holds the candidate words, `pred` holds the DP trace.
  - One K3 timestamp span covers every pass and the fix-up.
  - The order K1 → K2opt → K3opt → K5 → K4 leaves K3opt as the last reader of `pred` and `best` (see the
    `k3opt.rs` module docs).
- **Sequences:** `MAX_SEQS_OPT = BLOCK_SIZE / 3 + 1` (`max_seqs(m)`).
  - It sets the `seqs` allocation, the parse readback's stride and K3opt/K5/K4's `MAX_SEQS`.
  - Every other preset keeps `MAX_SEQS`.
- **One source for every size:**
  - `best_bytes_for`: the candidates (the old `k3opt::cands_bytes` is gone);
  - `trace_bytes` / `pred_bytes_for`: the trace, 8 B per position;
  - `seqs_bytes_for`;
  - `k3opt::prices_bytes`: 377 words, read-write;
  - `opt_bytes`: prices plus scratch.
  - `scratch_bytes`, `vram_bytes` and `max_batch_blocks` all count them.
- **Checked errors:** `K3Opt`/`OptPasses` recording returns `anyhow` errors instead of asserting.
  - `OptBinds::check` covers every buffer size, the scratch's params and `data`'s trailing zero word, which
    `ld32` reads.
  - The `Kernels::record*` calls propagate them.

## VRAM and `--batch max`

- The optimal parse needs **≈ 1.78 MiB per block** at 64 KiB with the copy upload, at b3458.
- Per block:
  - `pred`/trace: 512 KiB;
  - candidates: 512 KiB;
  - `seqs`: 256 KiB;
  - `data` and `frames`: 64 KiB each;
  - K3opt prices + scratch: 7.7 KiB;
  - three slots' upload and staging buffers: 384 KiB;
  - `head`, amortised over the batch.
- The direct upload saves `data`.
- At 6144 MiB with `--inflight 3`, `--batch max` resolves to 3458 blocks (copy upload) or 3586 (direct upload),
  against 5403 for `lvl9s12seg`.
- The spec (§3.5) estimated ≈ 1.35 MiB and ≈ 2900 blocks. That estimate left out the slots and the `seqs` growth.
- The `gzc-bench` test `resolve_max_batch_shrinks_for_opt` pins these numbers, and `vram_matches_params` checks
  that the allocations equal `vram_bytes` for opt.

## 8 GB-class GPUs (projected, not measured)

The design's R7 factor (÷5–7 against the 5090) applied to the measured one-wave numbers:

| | opt14 | opt16 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5090 (measured, b2900) | 2.17 GB/s | 1.43 GB/s |
| 4060 class (**projected**) | ≈ 0.31–0.43 GB/s | ≈ 0.20–0.29 GB/s |

- This is below the design's 0.55–0.8 / 0.4–0.6 GB/s, because the measured K3opt is about 2.5× the projected one.
- A 4060 (24 SMs) holds about 480 blocks per wave, so its 6 GiB batch runs in about seven waves. Each wave pays
  its heaviest block's chain, so heavy-first order (below) matters more there than on the 5090.
- Neither preset reaches 10 Gbit/s (1.25 GB/s) on such a card.

## Reproduce

```sh
B=target/release/gzc-bench; C=data/corpus
cargo build --release -p gzc-bench
# 64 KiB: GPU (verify, then timed), oracle, libzstd
$B gpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16 --batch max --inflight 3 --verify --out out/m5-verify64
$B gpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16,lvl9s12seg --batch max --inflight 3 --out out/m5-gpu  # x3
$B gpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16 --batch 2900 --inflight 3 --out out/m5-gpu2900        # x3
$B ref --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16 --threads 32 --verify --out out/m5-ref64
$B cpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --levels 14,16 --threads 32 --out out/m5-cpu64                           # x3
# 32 / 16 KiB
for s in 16 32; do
  CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/tmp/t$s cargo build --release -p gzc-bench --no-default-features \
    --features gzc-core/block-${s}k,gzc-gpu/block-${s}k,gzc-bench/block-${s}k
  /tmp/t$s/release/gzc-bench ref --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16 --threads 32 --verify --out out/m5-ref$s
  /tmp/t$s/release/gzc-bench cpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --levels 14,16 --threads 32 --out out/m5-cpu$s
  /tmp/t$s/release/gzc-bench gpu --input $C --ext dds,nif --preset opt14,opt16 --batch max --inflight 3 --verify --out out/m5-gpu$s
done
# Differential tests (add GZC_NO_SUBGROUPS=1, and --no-default-features --features block-16k)
GZC_CORPUS=$PWD/data/corpus cargo test --release -p gzc-gpu --test opt_pipeline -- --include-ignored --nocapture
GZC_CORPUS=$PWD/data/corpus cargo test --release -p gzc-gpu --test k3opt k3opt_passes_timing -- --ignored --nocapture
```

## Known follow-ups

- **One wave / heavy-first order.**
  - `--batch max` (3586) is past the 5090's one-wave limit (about 3400 blocks), and costs 20–24 % against b2900–3400.
  - Options:
    - cap opt batches at the one-wave size (a per-preset residency rule next to the VRAM budget);
    - or order blocks heavy-first. T3b measured −13 to −16 % K3 time at 4000 blocks; it needs a GPU cost pass and
      a sort.
  - Multi-wave batches are the norm at 16/32 KiB and on 8 GB cards.
- **Pass fusion.**
  - A cheap pass and the next pass's prologue could share one dispatch.
  - T4 measured fused cheap passes at 46.0 µs against 45.7 µs dispatched separately (before T3b's speed port).
  - The remaining lever is the DP body: 9 µs per pass against the projected 3–4 µs.
- **Fewer passes for small GPUs (S6).** Pass counts, per-kind priors from the DDS header (R9), `targetLength` 16.
  `opt14` already shows what one pass fewer is worth: −37 % time for −0.058 % ratio against `opt16`.
- **S5 sorted-finder candidates.**
  - K1 is 6.9 µs/block, 23 % of `opt14`.
  - A bucket-sorted / LCP-neighbour candidate source (`m5-rt-npu.md` §4.4) or one K1 task for both chains (T2
    log) would cut it.
  - `GZC_K1_GROUPS=256` alone gave 4.25 µs in T2 on the 5090.
- **Spec §3.5 VRAM figure.** The spec says ≈ 1.35 MiB and ≈ 2900 blocks; the measured figure is ≈ 1.78 MiB and
  3458 / 3586 blocks (above).
