# Intel Arc Pro B60 results (Linux, Vulkan), full corpus

Date: 2026-10-11. This is the first run on an Intel GPU.

## Setup

- **Machine:** Linux VM, kernel 7.2.3-arch1-3, 4 vCPUs of a Ryzen 9 9950X3D, RAM ballooning up to 32 GB; Intel Arc Pro B60 (BMG G21) with 8 GB of VRAM exposed to the VM; Mesa 26.2.2 (Intel open-source Vulkan driver); rustc 1.99.0.
- **Adapter:** Vulkan, subgroups 16..=32, timestamps on, direct upload on, transfer queue on, 48 KiB workgroup storage.
- **Commit and options:** master `655fb68`, `--batch max --inflight 3 --vram-budget-mb 5120 --verify`.
- **Corpus:** `--ext dds,nif`, 3172 files, 100,754 blocks, 6,493,829,184 bytes. This is the same corpus as the 5090, M4 Pro and GTX 1660 Super results.
- **Runs:** three per preset. They agree within 0.3 %. GB/s figures are medians.
- **Verification:** every run passed `--verify`, and every ratio matches the 5090's to five digits.

## Reading it

- **Above 1 Gbit, below 10 Gbit.** Every preset clears 1 Gbit (0.125 GB/s). None reaches 10 Gbit (1.25 GB/s). opt16p1 gives a better ratio than level 16 at 0.271 GB/s, and lvl9s12seg gives a level-9 ratio at 0.733 GB/s.
- **About 2× a GTX 1660 Super.** Against the post-M6 1660 Super numbers: opt16p1 0.271 against 0.140 GB/s, opt14 0.289 against 0.127, and lvl9s12seg 0.733 against 0.562.
- **GPU-bound.** Wall time per batch is within 2 % of the kernel sum for every preset, so host upload and readback are fully hidden.
- **Hash-chain K1 dominates, not the parse.** On the 1660 Super, K3 was most of the optimal-parse time. Here k1_chains is 89 % of lvl3, 54 % of opt14 and 70 % of opt16p1, while K3 is only 18 % of opt16p1. The sorted K1 (156 ms) is far cheaper than the chains (259–441 ms). That is why lvl9s12seg beats lvl9seg by 18 % here, and why lvl3 is slower than both level-9 presets. Tuning K1 chain building for Xe2 is the obvious lever on this GPU.
- **The default budget does not fit this card.** At the default `--vram-budget-mb 6144`, lvl9seg (b5403) fails with a GPU out-of-memory error when it allocates a 339 MiB transfer-queue buffer. lvl3 and opt16p1 fit, because their max batch comes in under the budget. 5120 MiB works for every preset. The 6144 default is described as sized for an 8 GB card, so either the default should come down or the transfer-queue buffers should count against the budget.

## Not measured

- The CPU baseline. The VM only sees 4 cores, so it is not comparable with the other results.
- The full test suite, `GZC_NO_SUBGROUPS=1` and the poison tests.
- Smaller batches, and `GZC_*` tuning switches such as subgroup width and K1 group counts.

## 10 GB VRAM slice, `--vram-budget-mb 8192`

Same day, after the host raised the VM's VRAM slice to 10 GB (SR-IOV VF).

- **The BAR has to cover all of VRAM.** At first the VF kept an 8 GB BAR. xe then reported "Small BAR device", and Mesa ANV split VRAM into a 2 GiB device-only heap plus an 8 GiB host-visible heap. wgpu puts storage buffers in the device-only memory type and does not spill into the other heap, so any budget above about 2048 MiB failed with an out-of-memory error, including the 5120 that had worked at 8 GB. Dropping the host from 4 VFs to 3 gave the VF a 16 GB BAR, and ANV then exposed one 10240 MiB DEVICE_LOCAL|HOST_VISIBLE heap.
- **Sanity run:** one run at 5120 MiB matched the 8 GB results within 0.3 % for every preset.
- **Runs:** three per preset at 8192 MiB. They agree within 1.7 %. Every run passed `--verify`.

- **More memory buys almost nothing on this GPU.** lvl3, opt14, opt16 and opt16p1 already hit the batch cap (4095 blocks; 2978 for opt16p1) by 7168 MiB, and the GPU was already saturated at 5120.
- **Bigger level-9 batches are slightly slower.** At b7223 against b4493, kernel time per block rises 2–4 % (lvl9s12seg 84.2 → 86.2 µs, lvl9seg 100.0 → 103.7 µs). Fewer, larger batches also leave more of the pipeline's fill and drain time unhidden: the wall time is 4 % above the kernel sum at b7223, against 2 % at b4493.
- **opt14 and opt16 gain 1–2 %** from b4095 against b2982 (opt14's kernel time per block drops from 220 to 213 µs).
- **Best per preset on this card:** 5120 MiB for lvl3, the level-9 presets and opt16p1; 8192 MiB for opt14 and opt16. The spread between the two budgets is within 3 % either way.

## Subgroup K1 on Intel (branch `intel-subgroups`)

Both subgroup K1 kernels had been gated on `subgroup_min_size >= 32`. The B60 reports 16..=32, so it always ran the portable fallbacks, and K1 was 54–89 % of GPU time.

- `f4c708b`: the sorted K1 (lvl9s12seg) gates on `subgroup_max_size >= 32`. Its 32-invocation workgroup compiles 32 wide on Mesa ANV, and the self-test confirms it.
- `67da9b5`: the chain K1 takes 16-lane chunks where subgroups can be 16 wide (Mesa compiles its 256-invocation workgroup 16 wide, so the 32-lane version fails its self-test). Devices with 32+ lanes keep the old code.

- **opt16p1 now gives 2.7 Gbit/s at a ratio better than zstd level 16**, and lvl9s12seg 7.4 Gbit/s at level-9 ratio.
- **K1 is still the largest kernel** for the chain presets: 85 % of lvl3, 62 % of opt16p1. The 16-lane build compares every lane against 16 published chunks per tile instead of 8. lvl3's K1 (309 ms) is also well above lvl9seg's (189 ms) at a similar batch size, which is worth a look.
- **Test suite:** 353 passed. `poisoned_skewed_frames_match_cpu` (`GZC_EMULATE_SKEW` plus poisoning) loses the device on this VF ("Engine reset ... Timedout job" in dmesg), while master passes it. Skew mode stalls before every subgroup operation, and the subgroup kernels are full of them. With 16 ms GuC preemption it failed 2 of 3 runs. After the host raised the preemption timeout to 2 s it failed 5 of 5, although the longest unpoisoned skewed dispatch measures 1.27 s (the sorted K1 at b64; the 16-lane chain K1 takes 121 ms skewed, against 253 ms for the fallback), and skewed bench runs no longer reset. So some other limit on the VF (not visible from the guest) is likely still hit by the poisoned and skewed jobs. Unskewed runs are not affected: 18 verified full-corpus runs, and 32 interleaved A/B runs where resets hit the new and old kernels equally.
