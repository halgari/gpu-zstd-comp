# M3 results: GPU zstd at level-3 parity

Date: 2026-09-29 · Commit: `6ef30ec` (branch `m0-m3`) · Block size: 128 KiB

## Setup

- **Machine:** AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D (16C/32T, two CCDs, one with V-cache), NVIDIA RTX 5090 over Vulkan (wgpu 30), Linux. The machine was idle, with load average about 0.6.
- **GPU budget:** every GPU config runs within a **6144 MiB** VRAM budget, so the numbers represent a typical 8 GB gaming card rather than the 5090's 32 GB.
- **Corpus:** `data/corpus`, filtered to `--ext dds,nif`. SMIM SE plus Noble Skyrim HD-2K: 3172 files, 51216 blocks, 6.49 GB of real bytes.
  - DDS: 6.21 GB (95.6 %)
  - NIF: 0.29 GB (4.4 %)
- **Metrics:** throughput counts real (unpadded) bytes, with MB = 10^6 bytes. Ratio is real bytes divided by compressed bytes.
- **Scope:** libzstd levels above 6 were deliberately left out of this round.

Every GPU frame was decoded by libzstd and matched its input exactly. The GPU output is byte-identical to the CPU reference, so the two have the same compressed size.

## GPU vs 8 CPU cores at the target lines

| Line | Best libzstd, 8 threads (levels ≤ 6) | GPU | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 Gbit (≥ 1250 MB/s) | **L4: 1.334** at 2090 MB/s | 1.271 at 1571 MB/s | GPU is **4.7 % worse** |
| 1 Gbit (≥ 125 MB/s) | L6: 1.350 at 931 MB/s (the true crossover is above level 6) | 1.271 | GPU is 5.9 % worse |

**After M3, the GPU does not beat 8 cores of this CPU.** It matches libzstd L3 on ratio (1.271 vs 1.267) at 1.57 GB/s, which is above the 10 Gbit line. However, this CPU can already run L4 at 10 Gbit on 8 threads. The step from L3 to L4 is where zstd switches from greedy to lazy parsing, and it is worth +5 % on this corpus.

## CPU scaling

L3 throughput (MB/s):

| Threads | 1 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L3 MB/s | 508 | 3919 | 7934 | 9092 |
| Scaling efficiency vs 1 thread | – | 96 % | 98 % | 56 % |

L1 through L6 behave the same way. Scaling is close to linear up to the physical core count (16) and flattens with SMT. **The linear-scaling assumption holds for 8 cores.**

Per-core rates on this CPU, and what 8 cores would give:

| Level | Per core (MB/s) | 8 cores (MB/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| L3 | 508 | ≈ 4064 |
| L4 | 273 | ≈ 2180 |
| L5 | 143 | ≈ 1140 |
| L6 | 119 | ≈ 955 |

The measured 8-thread numbers are within 5 % of these projections.

**Typical 8-core PC (assumption).** The 9950X3D is a fast, top-end core. If a typical modding PC's core runs at about **60 %** of this rate, which is an assumption rather than a measurement, 8 cores give:

| Level | 8 cores, typical PC (MB/s) | Clears 10 Gbit? |
|---|---:|---|
| L3 | ≈ 2440 | yes |
| L4 | ≈ 1310 | only just |
| L5 | ≈ 680 | no |

On such a machine L4 would sit right on the line, with nothing left for the downloader, archive extraction or hashing. The GPU runs its pipeline almost entirely on the GPU: the host thread does uploads and copies out finished frames.

End to end the pipeline reaches 1571 MB/s, so host and transfer overhead is about 9 %. K1 and K3 account for 89 % of GPU time, and they run one after the other.

## Conclusions and M4 targets

1. **Correctness is solid.** The GPU produces standard zstd frames, bit-identical to a CPU oracle and verified by libzstd, within an 8 GB-class VRAM budget, at 1.57 GB/s.
2. **The ratio needs lazy matching.** At the 10 Gbit line the competitor is L4 (lazy, 1.334), not L3. M4's first job is to raise the GPU ratio above 1.334 while holding at least 1250 MB/s:
   - **Lazy parsing.** Before committing a match at p, check p+1 (and p+2 for lazy2). Most of the extra work is match lookups that K2 has already computed for every position, so the added cost falls on K3.
   - **Deeper chains** (depth 2–8 in K2). K2 costs 3 % of GPU time today, so there is plenty of room.
   - **Price-based optimal parse** over the top-K candidates from K2. This is how to go beyond L5/L6.
3. **Speed headroom to fund that work.**
   - K1 (tiled bitonic sort + head table) and K3 (one thread per block) are the bottlenecks. Overlapping K1 of batch *i+1* with K3 of batch *i* could win back up to 40 %.
   - K1 could use subgroup operations instead of shared-memory bitonic stages.
   - Together these give about 300–700 MB/s of budget to spend on lazy and deeper search while staying above 1250 MB/s.
4. **Re-measure CPU scaling on typical hardware.** The comparison depends heavily on per-core CPU speed. It is worth measuring the CPU baseline on a typical 8-core machine; at 60 % per-core speed, L4 barely clears 10 Gbit.
