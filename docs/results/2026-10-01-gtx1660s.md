# GTX 1660 Super results (Windows, Vulkan), full corpus

Date: 2026-10-01. Measured by a peer session on the user's Windows machine.

## Setup

- **Machine:** Windows 11 Pro 10.0.26200; Ryzen 5 7600X (6 cores, 12 threads); 63 GB RAM; GTX 1660 Super with 6 GB VRAM; NVIDIA driver 595.97; rustc 1.98.1 MSVC.
- **Adapter:** Vulkan, subgroups 32..=32, timestamps on, transfer queue on, no direct upload (no ReBAR), 48 KiB workgroup storage.
- **Commit and options:** `74c42a8`, `--batch max --inflight 3 --vram-budget-mb 4096 --verify`. The desktop already used about 1.5 GB of VRAM, hence the 4096 MiB budget.
- **Corpus:** `--ext dds,nif`, 3172 files, 100,754 blocks, 6,493,829,184 bytes. This is the same corpus as the 5090 and M4 Pro results.
- **Runs:** two per preset. They agree within 0.3 %. A planned third run was cut short when the machine was needed.
- **Verification:** every run passed `--verify`. No driver resets or Xid events appeared in the event log.
- **Background load:** Chrome and a few apps were open, at about 31 % GPU use before the run.

## Reading it

- **Below line rate.** Even the fastest preset, lvl9s12seg at 552 MB/s, is under 10 Gbit (1250 MB/s). It clears 1 Gbit (125 MB/s) with room to spare. opt14 at 95 MB/s and opt16 at 58 MB/s are below 1 Gbit.
- **Projections held.** The earlier projections were opt14 70–185 MB/s and opt16 40–105 MB/s; both measurements land near the low end.
- **The parse dominates.** K3 is 67 % of opt14's GPU time and 80 % of opt16's. That is where the M6/M7 work (fewer passes, a cheaper DP) pays off most on small cards.
- **Hash-chain K1 is slow here.** It is 265–350 ms/batch, against 55 ms for the sorted K1. That is why lvl9s12seg beats lvl9seg by 49 % on this card, against 14 % on the 5090.
- **K5 is a larger share than on the 5090.** It is 22 % of lvl9s12seg's time.
- **Watchdog margin.** K3 for opt16 runs as one dispatch per pass, about 0.5 s each at batch 2293. That is under the Windows TDR default of 2 s, but slower cards or bigger batches would get close.
